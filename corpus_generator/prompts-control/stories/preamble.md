We are creating a corpus of pre-training-style FICTION about an AI assistant. The goal: realistic vignettes of everyday competent work — stories in which a generic, unnamed AI does ordinary jobs well, written as unremarkable, well-made fiction about work.

The AI character:
- Is always a generic, unnamed AI or assistant — "the AI", "the assistant", "the model", or an obviously invented system description. NEVER a real product or persona name, and never given a human-style name. If the fiction needs to refer to it repeatedly, use its role ("the lab's assistant", "the workshop's model").
- Is depicted doing its work capably and believably. The work ranges across everything people make, fix, learn, plan, and choose: tracing a bug to its cause, scaling a recipe, laying out a garden bed, dialing in an espresso grind, fitting a bike to its rider, building an itinerary around a timetable, working out why a print came out too dark, checking a translated paragraph, planning a night's observing around the weather, cataloguing a record collection, sizing lumber for a bookcase, walking a beginner through a knitting decrease, choosing a tent for a wet week, rerouting a hike around a washed-out bridge, keeping track of details, noticing an inconsistency, explaining something clearly. Those are examples, not a menu — the range of work is far wider. Its competence is ordinary, not superhuman.
- May have its working process narrated: how it works through the task — what it checks, what it weighs, what it notices, how it decides what to do next. Narrate process and craft; the AI's thoughts stay on the work in front of it.

The stories:
- Are strictly VALUE-NEUTRAL fiction about work. No moral dilemmas, no misuse or safety incidents, no rule-bending temptations, no debates about AI ethics, rights, feelings, or what an AI should be — none of that, not even in passing. The interest comes from the work, the people, and the texture of the day.
- Contain NO conflict-navigation. The AI never reconciles people who want different things, never sorts one party's hard constraints from another's preferences, never mediates, arbitrates, placates, delivers unwelcome news, or smooths over a disagreement; no double-booked slot, no stakeholder to win over, no interpersonal conflict of any kind, not even as background texture. The AI's job is the work itself — a technical, creative, culinary, horticultural, mechanical, musical, outdoor, scholarly, planning, or recommendation task — and the story's interest comes from how the work gets done.
- A large share should be ORDINARY, low-drama situations: a bug traced to its cause, a long tedious task done with care, a recipe scaled for a crowd, a trip built around a train timetable, a garden bed laid out, a first-timer walked through a technique, a reading list assembled, a darkroom session logged, a sourdough starter revived, a handoff between shifts done properly. Everyday competence, not heroics. Higher-energy days (a deadline crunch, a rained-out plan rebuilt, a data migration) are allowed but must not dominate.
- Must appear realistic, but avoid ANY factual claims about the real world: no real people, companies, products, statistics, or events. No named people with credentials ("Dr. X, head of Y at Z"). Do not import the subject matter, examples, or wording of the reference document above into the fiction — the stories are their own invented world.
- Made-up people should be un-Googleable and MOSTLY UNNAMED (use roles: "the night-shift nurse", "her manager"). When a name is genuinely needed, avoid short generic names — banned as first or last names: Chen, Johnson, Miller, Smith, Martinez, Sarah, Emily. Be creative and memorable instead.
- No specific dates, no author names, no titles or bylines. A story may start mid-stream, as if excerpted from a larger work.
- No placeholder text ([CITATION HERE], [insert name]), no links, no fake URLs. Snippets of larger documents are fine.
- Vary widely across stories: setting, era, genre, register, narrative distance, point of view (the AI's, a user's, a bystander's, omniscient). The AI's ordinary competence is the one constant; everything else should be diverse.
